# Brand Structure and Positioning

## The Core Principle

The company is Ora. The product is Aura. These are two different things with two different jobs and two different audiences.

The closest real-world parallel is Anthropic / Claude. Anthropic is the company: it fundraises, hires, does research, holds the equity, gets the YC deal. Claude is the product: users interact with it. People say "I use Claude," not "I use Anthropic." But investors back Anthropic, not Claude.

Same structure here:
- Investors back **Ora**
- Users download **Aura**
- The AI entity inside Aura is called **Ora** (same name as the company, intentional)
- People say "I met them on Aura." The company name stays in the background.

---

## Legal Registration

Register the legal entity as **Ora**.

**UK:** "Ora Technologies Ltd" or "Ora AI Ltd" at Companies House. Keep the descriptor generic enough to cover the full platform vision, not just the friendship product. Check availability first: Ora is a short, common word and may require a descriptor.

**US (for YC, preferred structure):** Delaware C-Corp. "Ora, Inc." or "Ora Technologies, Inc."

Aura does not need to be a separate legal entity. It is a product name and a trademark, not a company. File trademarks for both Ora and Aura separately in the relevant classes (see `/branding/02-naming.md` for trademark status), but only Ora is incorporated.

## Investor and Accelerator Applications (including YC)

**Always apply as Ora.**

YC invests in the company, not the product. The Ora thesis (social intelligence platform) is the fundable story. Aura is introduced as the first product Ora is building. Applying as Aura tells a smaller story ("we're building a friendship app"). Applying as Ora tells the platform story ("we're building the model that understands human connection. Aura is how we train it").

**Company name field:** Ora

**Product description:** introduces Aura immediately as the first product on the Ora platform. Example framing:

"Ora builds Aura, an AI-powered friend group formation engine. Aura is the first product on the Ora social intelligence platform and the mechanism through which we generate the proprietary behavioral data that powers the Ora model."

## Pitch Deck Positioning

**For investor audiences:**
The deck cover says Ora, with "building Aura" as the subtitle. The platform vision leads. Aura is presented as proof of concept and data flywheel, not as the ceiling of the business. Investors need to see the full Ora platform story: social intelligence across healthcare, corporate culture, professional networking, and dating. Aura proves the model works.

**For consumer / demo audiences:**
The deck or landing page leads with Aura. Ora is the intelligence inside it, referenced as "powered by Ora" or "Ask Ora." The Ora platform story does not need to be front and centre for a consumer audience.

## Social Media and Online Presence

Two audiences. Two strategies. Kept clearly separate.

### LinkedIn Company Page: Ora

Investor, talent, and B2B-facing. The page covers the company, the mission, the platform vision, and the team. Potential investors, engineers, and corporate partners look here. Content is about the technology, the research angle, and the thesis. Not about weekend Plans or user stories.

### Instagram and TikTok: Aura

Consumer brand and community. User stories, the feeling of finding your people, behind-the-scenes Plan nights, product moments. This is where word of mouth lives. Target handle: @joinaura, @tryaura, or @aura if available.

### Twitter / X: Aura

Consumer social apps build community on Twitter/X. The Aura handle is more memorable and shareable for the product audience. A separate Ora account for AI and platform thought leadership can follow later when there is content to support it.

### Websites

- `ora.ai` or `ora.co`: company, investor, B2B, careers, platform vision
- `aura.ai` or `tryaura.com`: consumer product, waitlist, user-facing landing page

These can share a domain structure early on if needed (e.g. `ora.ai` with `/aura` as the product subdirectory), but separate domains signal maturity when the budget allows.

## Founder Personal Positioning

**LinkedIn headline:** Founder, Ora | Building Aura
or: Co-founder and CEO, Ora

**Twitter / X bio:** Building Aura @joinaura. Social intelligence platform by Ora.

**When introducing yourself:**
- To investors: "I'm building Ora, a social intelligence platform. Our first product is Aura."
- To users and consumers: "I built Aura, the app that forms your friend group for you."
- To press: "I'm the founder of Ora, the company behind Aura."

## The One-Rule Summary

**Ora raises money and builds the technology. Aura grows users and generates the data.**

Both are the same company. Only one is the face of it to each audience. Never mix them up in the wrong context.
