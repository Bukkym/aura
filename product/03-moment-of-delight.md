# The Moment of Delight: Contextual Social Prescription

## The Concept

The core product emotion Aura is designed to produce is not "I found my people." It is: "this app went through real effort just for me, right now."

That feeling is specific. It requires Ora to know not just who someone is socially, but where they are in life at this moment: what they can afford, what kind of group they need today, and what hidden experience would genuinely surprise them in a way a generic recommendation never could.

This is contextual social prescription. Not "who should you meet" but "what does this specific person need right now, given their life stage, their energy level, their budget, and what would produce a moment of genuine delight."

---

## What Ora Needs to Know

### Life Stage Context

People do not exist in a static social mode. The same person needs completely different social experiences at different moments:

- New to a city, excited but slightly adrift: needs a mid-sized group, mid-energy activity, something that signals "this city has good people"
- Post-breakup or going through something hard: needs 3-4 people maximum, low-pressure, absorbing activity, warm-energy people
- Overwhelmed by work or life logistics: needs low-effort, high-reward, something that requires nothing of them except showing up
- Financially tight: needs curation that does not feel like a compromise. The free gallery opening with three compatible people can feel more special than an expensive dinner with people who do not fit

Ora learns life stage through two mechanisms:
1. A single, human-feeling question at onboarding: "What is your social life like right now, and what would make it better?" One answer. No form. Not clinical.
2. Behavioral inference over time: declining Plans, lower engagement signals, shorter responses. Ora reads the signals.

### Group Size Intelligence

Group size is one of the most underrated variables in social experience.

Someone who needs to process and connect needs 3-4 people. Intimate, specific, high-attention.
Someone who needs energy and stimulation needs 8-10. Dynamic, anonymous enough to relax into, social enough to feel alive.

Ora learns which mode works for each person from vibe feedback patterns. Not by asking. By watching which group sizes generated the strongest re-booking signals over time.

### Budget Awareness

Budget awareness is not about finding cheap options. It is about finding high-delight, low-cost options that still feel curated. The product should never make someone feel managed down to their budget. It should make them feel like Ora found something rare that happens to be accessible.

This requires:
- Explicit budget setting (optional, available in settings, no judgment framing)
- Behavioral inference from Plan acceptance patterns
- A curated inventory of low-cost, high-delight experiences: free cultural events, neighbourhood discoveries, off-peak venue deals, hidden spots that regulars know but visitors do not

The goal is that a person on a tight month has a Plan that feels more special than an expensive one would, because the curation is doing the work the price tag usually does.

### The Hidden Gem Layer

A significant part of the delight mechanic is discovery. Not just "compatible people" but "places and experiences you would never have found yourself."

This requires an active curation function, not just a recommendation engine. Someone needs to be finding the hidden wine bar that opens on Tuesday evenings for regulars. The free rooftop film screening. The neighbourhood restaurant that is only known by people who live nearby. The art studio that does drop-in sessions nobody advertises.

This layer is part product (surface these experiences in the Plan) and part partnership (relationships with venues who value curated introductions over mass marketing).

## The Delight Mechanics

### Curation Confidence

The product emotion of delight requires removing choice. Ora does not give someone five options and ask them to pick. It gives them one, occasionally two, with quiet confidence. The implicit message: "trust this."

The effort is invisible. The result feels personal. That gap, between unseen effort and felt personalisation, is the delight.

This is the opposite of most recommendation products, which show their working (here are 20 results based on your preferences). Ora shows only the output, not the process. The AI is invisible. The experience feels like magic.

### The Surprise Variable

Over time, Ora introduces deliberate surprise. Not random: calibrated. It has learned enough about someone to know when to match their stated preferences exactly and when to stretch them slightly in a direction the data suggests they will enjoy.

"You usually prefer smaller groups, but Ora thinks you will like this one. Here is why."

That moment, where the app correctly predicts something about you that you had not articulated, is the product inflection point. It generates the specific word-of-mouth moment: "I cannot explain how it knew, but it was exactly right."

### Proactive Care (Social Wellness Layer)

The most advanced version of the delight mechanic is proactive. Ora notices when someone has gone quiet: engagement has dropped, Plans are being skipped, response times are slowing. Rather than waiting for the user to re-engage, Ora surfaces something: a small Plan with one or two people from their existing group, something low-effort and specific to their current signals.

Not intrusive. Not a push notification saying "you seem lonely." Something quiet and concrete: "Ora thinks you and [name] should grab coffee this week. Here is somewhere nearby."

This is social wellness infrastructure. It does not exist anywhere today.

## How This Connects to the Ora Intelligence Vision

The delight mechanic is a product expression of Ora's deepest intelligence capability.

Phase 1 Ora (Listens): suggests Plans based on what you said. Gets the group roughly right. May not nail the context.

Phase 2 Ora (Watches): has seen your behavioral patterns. Knows your group size preference in practice. Knows which budget range you actually engage with. Starts adapting without being asked.

Phase 3 Ora (Knows): predicts what you need before you articulate it. Curates something you would not have thought to ask for but immediately recognise as exactly right. Produces the delight moment reliably.

The delight moment is the product proof that Ora's intelligence is real.

## Product Implications (Near-Term)

**Onboarding:** Add a single life stage question alongside the preference input. Human language, not a form. The answer seeds Ora's contextual model from day one.

**Vibe feedback:** Add a question about the experience itself, not just the people. "Did this feel like the right kind of thing for where you are right now?" That signal trains the contextual intelligence.

**Plan presentation:** Design for curation confidence. Never present more than two Plan options. Present them with a brief, specific rationale that makes the user feel seen, not sorted.

**Budget layer:** Add a quiet budget preference option in settings. No judgment framing. Surface it as "help Ora calibrate what to suggest."

**Hidden gem curation:** This is a product function that needs human input, at least initially. Someone needs to be actively discovering and tagging low-cost, high-delight experiences in each city the product operates in. Over time this can be systematised, but early on it is a curation job.

## The Experience Aura Is Competing With

Aura is not competing with other apps. It is competing with the feeling of a good friend who somehow always knows exactly where to take you, who to introduce you to, and when. Someone with great taste, real knowledge of your current situation, and no agenda. The product is this friend, made available to everyone.

Most social products feel like vending machines: you put in preferences and get out matches. Aura should feel like someone is actually thinking about you.
